This page is a list of any house rules or choices/clarifications I am using for our D&D game:

**Critical Hits**
* **Normal Rule**: When you roll a natural 20 on an attack roll, you double the *dice* you roll for your damage and add them all together, in addition to your other bonuses.
	* Example: Eredor normally deals 1d8+4 damage with his longsword. When Josh rolls a natural 20, he critically hits, and instead rolls 2d8+4 damage.
* **House Rule:** Instead of doubling the dice for your damage roll, roll your damage normally, and add an amount equal to a maximum roll of the dice to your result. Put another way, treat the "bonus dice" as having rolled the maximum result.
	* Example: Eredor normally deals 1d8+4 damage with his longsword. When Josh rolls a natural 20, he critically hits, and instead rolls 1d8+12 damage.
* This rule does not generally apply to monsters, but may so at the DM's discretion.
* *Notes: this rule is intended to ensure that critical hits are not disappointments, if you were to roll below-average on all your dice - e.g. rolling a 1 and a 2 on your 2d8+4. In future games, I might adjust this down to getting a flat "average" result instead of maximum - e.g. a critical hit with a 1d8 longsword gets +5 damage instead of +8 - still satisfying, but less swingy.*

**Heroic Inspiration**
* **Normal Rule:** The DM grants Heroic Inspiration ad hoc when they feel a player deserves it, whether through good roleplaying, good play, making everyone laugh, or otherwise.
* **House Rule:** If you roll a natural 1 on a d20 test *and* fail the test, you gain Heroic Inspiration after the action is resolved.
* **House Rule:** If you roll a natural 20 on a d20 test *and* succeed the test, you gain Heroic Inspiration after the action is resolved.
* This has to be the die actually used if you have Advantage or Disadvantage (e.g. if you roll with Advantage and get a 1 and a 13, it doesn't count).

**Potion Use**
* **Normal Rule in 5e 2014**: Drinking a potion or administering it to someone within reach requires an Action.
* **Normal Rule in 5e 2024**: Drinking a potion or administering it to someone within reach requires a Bonus Action.
* **House Rule**: Drinking a potion or administering it to someone within reach requires *either* a Bonus Action or an Action, but only one potion can be used on a turn in this way.
	* If you have an extra Action or Bonus Action from a spell or ability, like *Haste* or *Action Surge*, you can use them to exceed this limit.
* *Notes: this house rule looks complicated, but it's just about allowing players a little more flexibility without introducing unintended consequences.*
