
**Humans** have been updated.

* "Variant Humans" in 2014 got +1 to any two stats
* Humans now get either +1 to any *three* stats, or +2 to one stat and +1 to another stat.
	* In other words, you can increase any one stat of yours by 1

* Humans now gain **Heroic Inspiration** after every long rest. You can spend Heroic Inspiration to reroll any die. If you gain it and already have it, you can give it to another player who doesn't have it.

* Humans get a free ***Origin* feat** instead of *any* feat. This normally doesn't include Dual Wielder in the list. However:
	* **House Rule:** You can take Dual Wielder as your Origin feat, except you do not get the +1 to STR or DEX it offers (which is the same as the 2014 version, so no change here)
	* The new version of the feat doesn't grant +1 AC.

* The **dual-wielding** rules have been overhauled:
	* The *basic* version of the rules is the same: if you attack with a Light weapon, you can use a Bonus Action to attack with a different Light weapon, except the Bonus Action attack does not add your Ability bonus to the damage.
	* The *two-weapon fighting* Fighting Style works the same: it allows you to add your Ability bonus to the damage of your Bonus Action attack.
	* The *Dual Wielder* Feat is changed:
		* It grants +1 to STR or DEX (unless we use my house rule above)
		* It no longer grants +1 AC.
		* Only *one* of your weapons can lack the Light property, not both. So you can wield, say, a Longsword and Shortsword, but not two Longswords. You may swap any nonmagical weapons you like for free to accommodate this.
		* As written, it grants a *different* ability to attack using your Bonus Action, which can combine with the Nick mastery. I will explain more below.
#### Paladin Updates
* **Lay on Hands**:
	* The ability is now used as a Bonus Action instead of an Action. This applies whether you're using it on yourself or others.
	* Using 5 points of Lay on Hands can no longer cure disease, but can still cure the Poisoned condition.
* **Spells Prepared (part 1)** The number of spells you can prepare is now **fixed** (it used to be your level divided by two, plus your CHA bonus) and appears on the chart on page 110. The "free" spells you get from your Oath don't count towards that, just like before.
* **Weapon Mastery**: Pick two kinds of weapons. You can use the Mastery property of those weapons. You can change your choices after a Long Rest.
	* See below for my recommendation here, since you are a special case.
* **Fighting Styles** are now categorized as **Fighting Style Feats**, but they work the same way
* **Divine Smite**
	* Divine Smite is a **spell** now rather than a special ability.
	* You *always* have it prepared, and it doesn't count towards your Prepared Spells limit.
	* It's now a Bonus Action to cast it, but it (and *all* other Smite spells, e.g. Searing Smite, Thunderous Smite) can be cast reactively in the moment you hit something; you don't have to pre-cast them.
	* You need to prepare any other Smite spells you want to use, of course, but they're far more viable now, so I encourage you to check them out to see if you find them appealing.
* **Channel Divinity:** You now have two uses of Channel Divinity instead of one, and you get back 1 use on a Short Rest, and all back on a Long Rest.
* **Divine Sense** is now a function of Channel Divinity rather than a separate ability.
* **Faithful Steed:** You *always* have the Find Steed spell prepared, and it doesn't count against your limit, AND you can cast it once per day without using a spell slot.
	*  As a **House Rule**, you may summon the creature as a Medium size one instead of a Large one (the better to maneuver in combat), and have it serve you as a combat pet. Note that you don't have to summon it every day, so your one-free-cast per day means you can re-summon it after it dies once per day for free. It's not terribly powerful, but it can do some damage and you're entitled to it!
* **Spells Prepared (part 2):** This means that as a level six Oath of the Ancients paladin, you have twelve spells prepared total - Divine Smite and Find Steed, plus four Oath Spells - Ensnaring Strike, Speak With Animals, Misty Step, and Moonbeam. You can select six more of your choice from the paladin list on page 112.
* **Oath of the Ancients:** Your Channel Divinity is changed - you should read it to make sure you understand it. It is an improved, AOE version of your previous Nature's Wrath ability. You no longer have the Turn the Faithless ability.

#### Dual-wielding complications and rules updates

* Normally, I have been deliberately avoiding making optimization recommendations for players in this game. However, the 5.5 update impacts your character particularly hard - Divine Smite now competes for your Bonus Action with dual-wielding, and the way to optimize that is very unintuitive. Also, they removed the ability to dual-wield big weapons; one of them has to be Light now.
* **If you want to keep to as close to your current style as possible, I will help you choose the right combination of options and retroactively modify the weapons you're using. If instead you want to switch to a different fighting style (e.g. two-handed weapons or sword-and-board), I will do the same.**
* Important to your choice is the new **Weapon Mastery** system, and the **Nick** property specifically. If at least one of your two weapons has the Nick property, you can make the extra attack with it *without* using your Bonus Action.
* **Possible Options:**
	* 1 - **Switch to one smaller weapon and keep the Dual Wielder feat:** Instead of using two Longswords, you would use a Longsword and your choice of a Light weapon, such as a Shortsword. You would have to use your Bonus Action to make your extra attack, which would mean you could not Smite that turn.
	* 2 - **Switch to two smaller weapons, and drop the Dual Wielder feat, and one of the smaller weapons has the Nick mastery property:** Dual Wielder doesn't give you an AC bonus anymore, so if you use two smaller weapons, you could drop the feat. If you wield a weapon that has the Nick property, you could make all your attacks as part of your Action, leaving your Bonus Action free for Divine Smite or other uses.
	* 3 - **Switch to two smaller weapons, *keep* the Dual Wielder feat, and one of the smaller weapons has the Nick mastery property**: As written the extra attack granted by Dual Wielder is *different* from the inherent one granted by the Light property. Thus, if you have the Dual Wielder feat, both your weapons are Light, and one of them has the Nick property, you can make *two* extra attacks per turn, one of which uses your Bonus Action. As a level 6 Paladin, that means that with the Extra Attack ability, you would attack four times total - three times with your Action, and once with your Bonus Action. However, you could only make use of the Bonus Action attack if you don't use Divine Smite, so the benefit you get from your feat is fairly limited.
	* My personal *recommendation* is to go with option 2 - use, say, a scimitar and a shortsword. You will have the same number of attacks as you did before, but you will not have to use your Bonus Action to make them, and your Feat is freed up to select something else.
	* However, I understand that the *fantasy* of dual-wielding longswords is lost. So if you want to switch to a different fighting style *entirely*, I will support that decision.
	* Whatever you select, we will retcon your magic weapons to fit your choice.
